# North Carolina development coverage

Researched October 25, 2026. **80 distinct records: 20 each in Chapel Hill, Raleigh, Durham, and Greensboro.** This expansion adds 64 records to the previous 16. It is a curated snapshot, not an exhaustive inventory or automatic live feed.

## Carolina North and the forest

The Carolina North entry focuses on UNC’s proposed 230-acre former-airport development, with research/academic space, housing, commercial uses, and civic amenities. It explains the relationship to the larger property and forest, UNC’s stated protection of most forest land and trails, limited potential nearby trail impacts, the unsettled arena question, and the preliminary summer 2027 groundbreaking estimate. It does not label the whole forest as a building site, draw a fictitious boundary, or count potential campus buildings as multiple projects. Main sources: [UNC plans](https://carolinanorth.unc.edu/), [UNC FAQ](https://carolinanorth.unc.edu/frequently-asked-questions/), and [master-planning procurement](https://facilities.unc.edu/advertisements/designer-solicitations-carolina-north-carolina-north-campus-mixed-use-district-and-infrastructure-master-planning/). The former airport’s published reference coordinate is an approximate locator, not a surveyed development footprint. No final partner award is asserted from older projected selection dates in the FAQ.

## How records were selected

- Chapel Hill: individual Town project records and current formal-application/final-plan descriptions. Concept-only proposals remain Proposed, with their age/uncertainty explicit. Later phases are distinguished from completed earlier phases. The old Tarheel hotel is excluded from its Phase II entry. The new Cedars condominium and Carraway Phase III do not stand in for already existing neighborhoods.
- Raleigh: individual Downtown Raleigh Alliance project pages and its announced/construction inventory, cross-checked with City and state updates for major civic projects. The old 1313 Hillsborough apartment concept was rejected because other evidence identifies a restaurant redevelopment. Heritage Park replaces it and is conservatively marked Approved for financing; the Alliance’s site-preparation label does not establish that all buildings have started. Announced private projects have no independently confirmed permit/start dates and say so.
- Durham: official City–County planning case GIS, using exact application IDs, descriptions, status dates, and source points. Under Review and Corrections Required map to In review. These labels describe the cited application, not every permit on the site. All 20 Durham locations were checked against the [published City boundary service](https://services2.arcgis.com/G5vR3cOjh6g2Ed8E/ArcGIS/rest/services/Administrative/FeatureServer/1). Airport Road Light Industrial, Brightwood, and Project Lloyd were rejected because their points fell outside that boundary; Strauss Drive Townhomes, World Trade Multifamily, and NCSSM Commons replace them. Durham GIS summaries lack street addresses: entries use a clearly labeled case locator and link to the exact case/map rather than inventing an address.
- Greensboro: City-issued 2026 building permits for substantial development and adaptive reuse, plus the existing four primary-sourced projects. Selected permit records have Issued status and no recorded final CO or cancellation in the checked data. Permits are evidence of authorization, not independent construction observation. Repeated permits at Liberty View/Revolution, Guilford College Road, Comet Lake Jeanette, Mitchell Water Plant, and the Science Center are grouped into one record per development. No individual-house permits, signage, door replacements, or routine repairs were used to reach the count. The Science Center contractor’s current progress report separately confirms construction.

## Status and location limits

The public viewer keeps the four requested chips: Proposed, In review, Approved, Under construction. Approved may refer to zoning, an issued building permit, or conditional financing; the description explicitly states which. Chapel Hill’s combined “zoning compliance/permitting/construction” bucket is not automatically treated as active construction. Design approval for Smoky Hollow Park is distinguished from its later construction schedule; the latest City overview/schedule is preferred over older contradictory sections on the same page.

Pins come from Town project maps, the Alliance’s published map coordinates, official Durham/Greensboro GIS, and the Census/source coordinates documented in the first batches. All pins are approximate project locations, not parcel boundaries, entrances, or proof of jurisdiction. Durham boundary checks apply to the point, not a survey of every parcel. Unknown counts/developers/dates remain unconfirmed, and hotel rooms are not counted as homes. Some entries cover campus additions, adaptive reuse, parks, schools, or infrastructure rather than new housing.

Every record has source links and a research date. No public meeting dates were invented. The previously verified November 18, 2026 Council hearing for 306 West Franklin is retained; other notices without verified venue/access details remain links rather than agenda events. Confirm official agendas before attending.

## Preservation and verification

The operator import validates the full batch before opening the database, creates a private SQLite backup, adds only missing IDs, and preserves existing editor changes, accounts, follows, and history. New imports use an explicit operator audit identity instead of attributing automated research to the administrator. Existing fictional examples remain archived with their history. New installations receive the same 80 portable records.

The web and native map/editor design remains intact; city and category metadata are searchable, without restoring the removed city/category filter rows. A downloadable CSV register accompanies the source. Private environments, databases, and backups are excluded from the source archive.
